Asthma is a respiratory condition where airways become narrow, inflamed, or produce excess mucus. This makes breathing difficult and can cause wheezing, coughing, and chest tightness. Some asthma attacks are triggered by dust, cold weather, smoke, or exercise.

Children and adults with asthma should keep rescue inhalers available at all times. Proper inhaler technique ensures that medication reaches the lungs effectively. Inhaling too quickly or incorrectly can reduce the medicine’s impact.

Regular monitoring helps identify patterns and triggers. Patients may benefit from lifestyle adjustments such as avoiding allergens, reducing physical stress, or using air purifiers. A healthcare professional may prescribe daily preventive inhalers if symptoms occur frequently.

During severe episodes, hospitalization may be required if breathing difficulty does not improve with initial medication. Staying calm and breathing slowly can help reduce panic during an asthma attack. Early treatment and awareness can improve long-term outcomes for individuals living with asthma.
